The build

Nine automations, ranked by your priorities

Reordered to match the priorities Lauren and Paul sent on Aug 13. Each one is deterministic software: AI reads the unstructured documents, structured code produces the output, so nothing gets reworded, reformatted, or invented. Your top three (tracking, calendaring, the deadline digest) are really one engine, fed by the litigation-event emails that already hit your inbox. Every badge shows what it watches, when it fires, and where the human gate sits.

Ranked by you · Aug 13 follow-up
1

Portfolio Case Tracker

Your #1. Replaces the shared spreadsheet with a live portfolio view driven by the litigation-event emails you already receive: complaint filed, answer served, discovery served, MSJ filed. Each event advances the matter's state on its own across active, default, post-judgment, and sister-state. Nobody updates it; it stays current. Your working spreadsheet is the starting model.

reads litigation-event emailsalways currentgate: read-onlypilot
2

Calendaring & Document Saving

Watches the mailbox where served documents arrive as email attachments, saves each to the matter, and calendars the deadline through Clio so its date-calculation rules do the math. Same engine as the build underway with Josh, tuned to your attachment-based service. The human-error window on calendaring closes.

watches service mailfires on attachmentgate: exceptions flaggedpilot
3

Cost Accounting in Clio

Reads the vendor's monthly invoice and per-filing receipts, splits the charges by case, and posts them into Clio through its API, so costs land on the client invoice next to your fees. The paralegal's spreadsheet step disappears, roughly three hours a month returned.

watches cost emailsfires on invoicegate: monthly summarypilot
4

Deadline & Hearing Digest

New from your follow-up. A weekly digest of every upcoming deadline and court hearing, pulled from the Clio calendar, so nothing sneaks up. One email, every week, per attorney.

reads Clio calendarweekly digestgate: informationalpilot
5

Default Judgment Package Builder

Fills the court forms (request for entry of default, proposed judgment, request for dismissal) field by field, no flattening, and merges the declaration from the matter data. Built from the filed complaint plus the filing and service (POS) emails. Your exemplar package is in hand, and this is exactly where your Clio project stalled on the forms.

reads complaint + POS emailsfires on missed answergate: attorney reviewpilot
6

Task Lists & Reminders

New from your follow-up, and you called it huge. When an attorney emails a paralegal an assignment, it generates a shared task list and sends the reminders. Assignments stop vanishing into a black hole, and the supervising attorney doesn't have to remember to circle back. Attorneys get their own list too.

What stays yours

The system removes the typing, not the judgment

Lauren's frustration with raw Claude — rewording things, adding ten words where one would do — is exactly why this isn't a chatbot. Templates stay verbatim; only the fields change. And the calls that matter stay with the people who should make them.

✍️

Attorney signs every filing

Complaints, discovery, default packages, motions — nothing reaches a process server, a clerk, or opposing counsel without a one-look approval.

🤝

Settlement terms are yours

You negotiate; the email memorializes it. The system only turns terms you already agreed into a draft on the client's form.

⚖️

Strategy calls stay human

Whether to chase attorney's fees, when to push an MSJ, how hard to go post-judgment — the system queues the work; it never makes the call.

🚩

Exceptions flag, not file

A packet with missing documents, a mismatched contract form, an unusual guaranty — anything that fails a check stops and asks, instead of guessing.
