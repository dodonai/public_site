The stuck point

Judge and district templates, solved first

Bryn, 5/18: "we have a folder for the judges … that's a big part of what the paralegal does." This is the litigation-specific piece, and it is the knowledge most at risk in a seat change.

⚖️Which template applies to this case?cluster C · weeks 1 to 3
Today
  • Complaint filed; judge unknown until the ECF assignment email arrives.
  • Paralegal reads the email, opens the judge folder, picks the scheduling-order and filing templates by memory.
  • Southern and Middle District differ; each judge may add their own rules on top of local rules.
→
With the OS
  • ECF assignment email is read; court, judge and magistrate are written to the Service.
  • Your judge folders become data: the JSR (or the Middle District CMR) and its proposed order use the template the court names, else the judge's own form, else the firm's default form. Every date is computed from that judge's anchor dates (for example, trial so many days after the dispositive-motion deadline).
  • A wrong pick is caught at review, as today; Sonya checks the resolved set once per case instead of remembering it.

The method

SOP first, then build

Wave 1 built first and wrote the staff SOP afterwards. Litigation flips that. Each cluster gets a pre-build SOP written with Sonya and a litigation attorney, ratified by Bryn, then built to and accepted against. The same file becomes Sonya's training manual on day one. The October 5 walkthrough already answered most of the attorney-side questions; new workflows are in bold below.
