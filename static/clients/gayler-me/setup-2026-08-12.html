What we built for you

Louisiana Medical Review Panel Submission

The Main Deliverable

Your panel submission, assembled from the whole case file — the complaint, the medical records, and the depositions — and written in your six sections. It reads as an advocacy document: it argues your case from the record, rather than hedging.

The six sections it produces:

  • Statement of the CaseIntroduces the patient, the defendant provider(s) and facility, and the core allegations and claimed harm — drawn from the complaint.
  • Summary of Medical RecordsThe course of care in date order — complaints, findings, treatment, provider changes, and turning points — each anchored to its date and provider. The factual spine.
  • Pertinent Deposition TestimonyThe deposition admissions and testimony most material to the case, attributed to each witness, with page-line references preserved.
  • Standard of CareWhat a reasonably prudent provider of the same specialty should have done, grounded in the records and testimony.
  • Violations of the Standard of CareEach specific breach, listed one at a time, with the responsible provider named and the record or testimony that establishes it cited.
  • Causation of Damages / InjuriesTraces each breach to the resulting injury and downstream damages, argued from what the file supports, with the nature and severity of the injuries summarized.

Medical Records Chronology

Feeds Your Submission

Turns each medical record into one dated, plain-facts line — who treated the patient, where, and what happened clinically — in time order. This is what fuels the "Summary of Medical Records" section and gives the standard-of-care and causation analysis the clinical detail it needs.

What we pull out of each record:

  • DateWhen the visit, test, procedure, or clinical event happened.
  • ProviderThe treating physician, nurse practitioner, or PA — not lab or clerical staff.
  • FacilityThe hospital, clinic, imaging center, or practice where care was given.
  • Clinical SummaryA short, neutral 2–4 sentence account: the complaint, exam and test findings, treatment, and diagnoses — straight from the record.
Example entry
03/14/2025 — Dr. Jane Springer (Advanced Surgical): Patient presented with worsening right-lower-quadrant pain; exam and CT consistent with acute appendicitis. Laparoscopic appendectomy ordered and performed the same day. Discharged in stable condition with follow-up in one week.

Med Mal Panel Submission

Processing Rules

This is the setup you'll select when you run the summary — it's what tells Dodonai how to handle a Louisiana panel case. Behind the scenes, it sorts and labels every document so the submission builds from a clean, dated record, and it summarizes each part the right way — medical records into the chronology above, depositions into page-line summaries, and the complaint read straight into your Statement of the Case.

How your documents are organized:

  • Sorted byDate, earliest first, so the record reads as a clean timeline.
  • Grouped byDocument type — complaint, medical records, depositions — then by date within each group.
  • Index columnsDocument Title, Date, Provider, Facility.

The document categories we built:

  • Complaint / Panel RequestThe complaint, petition, or Patient's Compensation Fund panel request that lays out your allegations.
  • Medical RecordClinical records — office and ER visits, operative reports, imaging, labs, and discharge summaries.
  • DepositionSworn deposition transcripts of the defendant provider(s) and witnesses.

Blank pages, cover sheets, fax transmittals, and dividers are set aside automatically so they don't clutter your submission.

How to use it

  1. In a matter, select your case file — the complaint, the medical records, and the deposition transcripts.
  2. Click Summarize and choose Custom Summaries → Index-Based.
  3. Under Processing Rules, pick Med Mal Panel Submission (the setup we configured for you), make sure Executive Summary is turned on, and start it.
  4. When it finishes, open it from the matter's Summaries list — your six-section panel submission is the executive summary.
