The mechanism

What the email agent actually does

Not autocomplete, and not a rules-based filter that breaks the first time a sender changes their subject line. It reads for meaning, in the context of your matters, the way a good assistant would.

▶Six things it does on every passthe core
→Reads on a schedule, not just when you open the app. It runs overnight and through the day, so the inbox is already triaged before you look.
→Reads the whole thread, not the subject line. It follows the conversation and ties it to the matter it belongs to, so it routes on what the email means, not on keywords.
→Sorts into three tiers: needs-you-now, route-to-the-team, and routine / FYI. The inbox collapses from "everything" to the handful that need a decision.
→Delegates with context: the right person on your team gets the thread, the matter, and what is being asked, instead of a bare forward that starts them from zero.
→Drafts the routine replies: records acknowledgments, scheduling confirmations, standard follow-ups, written in your firm's voice and queued for a one-click approval.
→Surfaces only the few that genuinely need a lawyer, and leaves the rest handled. You spend your attention on judgment, not sorting.

Kills: the hour you lose every morning reading routine mail to find the two things that actually needed you.

You set the boundary

How much it does is a dial you control

The first question everyone asks is "how much am I handing over?" The answer is: exactly as much as you want, and you can start conservative and open it up as you trust it. Three common settings.

Read & sort only
most cautious

The agent triages and labels the inbox into tiers and flags who each thread belongs to. It writes nothing.

Good for: the first few weeks, while you watch how it classifies and confirm it gets your practice right.

Sort + route + draft
most common

It also delegates to the team and writes the routine replies, which sit in a queue for your one-click approval before anything sends.

Good for: the day-to-day once you trust the sorting. This is where most firms land, and where the time actually comes back.

Per-category rules
fine-grained

You decide it by type: auto-draft records acknowledgments, but only sort anything involving a client or opposing counsel. Your rules, per category.

Good for: firms that want the routine stuff fully handled but a tighter leash on anything sensitive.

There is no setting where an email leaves the firm on its own. Every one of these keeps a person on anything outbound. The dial only changes how much drafting and routing the agent does before you look.

What stays yours

The agent removes the sorting, not the judgment

This is the part that matters for a law firm. Automation stops where judgment starts, by design.

👤

Anything that leaves the firm

No reply, letter, or email out goes without a person's review. The agent drafts and queues; you approve. Nothing AI-written reaches a client or opposing counsel without sign-off. This is the direct answer to the hallucination worry.

🔀

Ambiguous threads

When the agent is not sure who a thread belongs to or how it should be handled, it flags it for a person instead of guessing. It never routes into the wrong hands to look busy.

⚖️

Legal judgment

What a message means for a case, and what to do about it, stays with your attorneys. The agent handles the triage and the first draft; the decision is yours.

⌨️

You own the system

This runs as AI internal to your firm that you own, not a subscription that disappears. The firm keeps the system, the rules, and the data.

Built for confidential work

Where your email actually goes

The agent runs in your own tenant. Your email and matter data are not pooled with anyone else's and are not used to train outside models. Encrypted in transit and at rest.
