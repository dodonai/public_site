Editing your website with Claude

Loke Moore Law

You talk to Claude in plain English and it does the careful part. Every change is shown to you before it goes live, checked so it can't break the site, and can be undone. You don't need to know any code, and you can't break anything by asking.

🔖 Bookmark this page. It lives on the web, not on your laptop, so you can open it from your phone or any computer even if your MacBook is in the shop. It is the current version; when something changes, it changes here.

1 Open the right folder first

In Claude, open the folder that matches what you want to change:

lokemoorelaw

Your website, the intake form's question wording, and this year's Medicaid dollar figures.

lokemoorelaw-pipeline

The planning letter's wording and the worksheet's labels.

2 Just say what you want

Take down the broken webinar link on the Helpful Links page.

Fix Angie's bio, it shouldn't say she just joined; she's been here for years.

Reword the "Relationship to client" question to "Relationship to the client."

Soften the second paragraph of the planning letter.

Update this year's Medicaid figures.

3 What always happens before anything goes live

  1. Claude shows you exactly what it will change: the wording before, and after.
  2. You read it and say yes. Nothing publishes until you do.
  3. It checks the change doesn't break the site, then publishes it for you.
  4. It opens your live page and finds your new words there before it tells you it's done.

Saving is not publishing. Claude saves a change to your files first, then runs a separate publish step that pushes it to the live website. Those are two different things, and a change that is saved but not published will never appear on lokemoorelaw.com.

Step 4 above exists to catch exactly that. If Claude ever tells you a change is done, always take the extra ten seconds to load the real page and look. If your words aren't there, say "that isn't live yet, please publish it" and it will finish the job.

4 The two lists that keep you safe

✓ You can freely ask for

  • Change any page's text or fix a typo
  • Fix or remove a link
  • Swap out an image
  • Take a page down
  • Reword an intake question
  • Update the yearly figures

! Claude will say no, call Nick

  • Renaming an answer choice (like "Married")
  • Changing a dollar figure by editing a document
  • Page layout or design changes
  • Adding a brand-new page
  • Touching any math or formulas

A "no" is Claude protecting you. These can quietly break the automation.

Undo

If a change ever looks wrong after publishing, just tell Claude "undo that last change" and it brings the previous version right back. Every change is saved. Nothing is ever truly lost.

5 Yes, they're called skills

A skill is a written instruction sheet that teaches Claude how to do one job for you safely. You have four. They live inside the two folders themselves, which is why Claude already knows them the moment you open the right folder.

SkillWhat it doesFolder
edit-website Change page text, fix or remove a link, swap an image, take a page down, and publish it live. lokemoorelaw
edit-intake-questions Reword a question on the Medicaid intake form without disturbing the answers behind it. lokemoorelaw
update-medicaid-figures Update this year's Medicaid dollar figures everywhere they appear. lokemoorelaw
edit-worksheet-letter Change the planning letter's wording and the worksheet's labels. lokemoorelaw-pipeline

If you ever ask Claude to improve one of these instruction sheets, that is a good thing, not a problem. They are meant to get better as you use them. Just tell me what you changed so I keep my copy in step with yours.

6 If your laptop stops working

Nothing important lives only on your MacBook. Each time a change is published, both folders are saved to a private archive on GitHub that you have your own login to. Here is the honest division of labor:

  • Safe Your website, your intake form, the planning letter, the worksheet, and every skill above. All of it is in the archive, including the full history of every change you've ever made.
  • Safe This page. It is on the web, so it survives your laptop.
  • My job Getting a new machine ready. The archive holds your files, not the tools that read them. A replacement laptop needs Claude, a few developer tools, and your Google sign-in before the folders are useful again.

So the short answer to "do I need Nick to reinstall this?" is yes, and it's about an hour of my time, not a rebuild. Nothing is lost and nothing gets recreated from scratch. Your live website keeps running the entire time. It doesn't live on your laptop either.

If Claude asks you to sign in

Once in a while your Google sign-in expires. If a change won't publish and it mentions signing in, paste these into Claude's prompt one at a time (the ! runs it for you), then try again:

# reconnect to Google Cloud
! gcloud auth login
! gcloud auth application-default login
! gcloud config set project lokemoorelaw-medicaid

Still stuck after that? That's a call-Nick moment, not something you did wrong.
