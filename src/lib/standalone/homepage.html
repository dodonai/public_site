What do you actually need from our people?

Time from the people who do the work, and not much of it. The Blueprint runs on interviews; after that the build is on us. We also need access to the work your firm has already approved, because a system that has never seen your standards cannot meet them.

How long until something is actually running?

The Blueprint takes one to two weeks. A first automation goes live in weeks rather than quarters, and the exact timeline is in the Blueprint before you approve it. If we cannot say when it will be live, we have not understood the work well enough to build it.

Do we have to leave the software we already use?

No. We build into the case management, email, document, and billing systems you already run, and bridge the ones without an API. Adoption fails when the tool sits outside the workflow, so we put it inside.

Where does our data go, and does it train anything?

Your systems of record stay where they are, and the environment we build runs in infrastructure you control. Generating output means sending the relevant material to the model providers you approve, under your agreements with them. Your work product is never used to train models for anyone else.

What happens when the output is wrong?

A workflow that fails a defined check stops and routes for review rather than proceeding, and consequential output waits for a person at the firm to approve it. Corrections feed the structured record, so the same mistake gets rarer instead of recurring.

What does it cost?

Three things, matching how we work: a fixed fee for the Blueprint, a fixed Build price per automation approved before development starts, and a monthly Run fee for operating and improving what is in production. Model usage is passed through at cost. Exact numbers come with your Blueprint.

What if we want to stop?

You keep the system. Full source, running in infrastructure you control, no long-term contract to exit. Stopping after one automation that works is a perfectly good outcome.
